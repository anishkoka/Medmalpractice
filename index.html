The Medical Malpractice Climate, by State

A composite score (0–100) built from three primary-source components: damage cap structure, NAIC-reported direct premium burden per physician, and litigation environment. Every state has primary-source measurement; no inferred values.

Favorable — 0–25
Mixed — 26–45
Hostile — 46+

Scoring Methodology

Each state receives a score 0–75 from three primary-source components. A lower score is friendlier to physicians.

1. Cap Structure · 0–35 pts

0 = strong cap (≤$500K) in force
10 = moderate cap or PCF state
20 = weak/eroded cap
30 = no cap (struck or never enacted)
35 = no cap with constitutional barrier

2. NAIC Premium Burden · 0–25 pts

2024 NAIC direct premium written per physician
0 = below $5,500 · 5 = $5.5–7.5K
10 = $7.5–10K · 15 = $10–13K
20 = $13–16K · 25 = above $16K

3. Litigation Environment · 0–15 pts

2025-26 ATRA Judicial Hellholes ranking
0 = no notable concerns
5 = ATRA Watch List
10 = ATRA Hellholes #5–8
15 = ATRA Hellholes #1–4
Bucket thresholds: 0–25 = Favorable · 26–45 = Mixed · 46+ = Hostile
Key innovation: Premium burden now measured directly via NAIC for all 51 jurisdictions, replacing earlier inferred estimates.
Sources: NAIC Countrywide Summary of Medical Professional Liability Insurance, Calendar Years 2010–2024 (state-by-state direct premium data through calendar year 2024) · AAMC 2023 State Physician Workforce Data Report (active physicians by state) · ATRA Judicial Hellholes 2025–2026 Report · NCSL state damage cap registry. Premium-per-physician computed as NAIC 2024 DPW ÷ AAMC 2023 active physicians. NAIC publishes annually; map updates whenever new vintage is released.
